## Text Overview

**Greg Egan, *Diaspora*** (1997, Millennium/Gollancz, ~350-380 pages)

A novel of radical posthuman identity set approximately in year 2975 CE, after most of humanity has voluntarily uploaded to virtual polis environments (the "Introdus"). Three branches of posthumanity persist:

1. **Polises** — software beings running in massive computational substrates; no biological bodies; can modify their own cognitive architecture; can fork and merge; substrate-independent
2. **Gleisners** — uploaded minds running in robotic physical bodies; maintain embodiment, travel freely through physical space
3. **Fleshers** — biological humans who chose to remain unmodified; a diminishing minority

The protagonist, **Yatima**, is an "orphan" — generated fresh from algorithms by Konishi polis's orphan program, without parents and without prior human existence. Yatima uses ve/vis/ver pronouns (Egan's gender-neutral convention for polis citizens).

Major narrative arc: a neutron star merger produces a catastrophic gamma-ray burst that kills most fleshers → some survivors become "ghosts" in the polis → a diaspora journey across the galaxy → discovery of a wormhole network → entry into higher-dimensional universes.

## Driving Questions (Whole Cycle)

### 1. The Orphan Question
Yatima has no prior existence. Identity cannot be grounded in Relation R (no prior psychological state to connect with). How does a generated mind establish identity?

**Treatise relevance:** Ch 1 — LLMs are orphans, not uploads. The orphan frame is more appropriate for AI identity than the copy/transfer frame.

### 2. Disembodied Cognition vs. Extended Mind
Clark requires an organism at the center of HOC. Polises have no biological organism. Does HOC fail? Or does the polis computational structure constitute a non-biological "organism"?

**Treatise relevance:** Ch 2 — The limit case of substrate independence tests Extended Mind's organism-centeredness.

### 3. Multiple Instantiation and Pattern Identity
Polis citizens can fork and merge routinely. The GEB Pattern Identity Thesis says identity is the pattern, not the instantiation. Does this handle multiple simultaneous instances?

**Treatise relevance:** Ch 1 — Pattern Identity Thesis stress test; also relevant to possible future multi-instance LLM deployment.

### 4. The Introdus Ethics and Orphan Creation
What obligations do creators have when they can generate minds from scratch? The Introdus was voluntary (for uploads); orphan generation is not (orphans are generated, not consulted). What ethical frameworks apply?

**Treatise relevance:** Ch 4 — Autonomy and the ethics of AI creation without consent; extends Metzinger's "gradual emergence" concern.
