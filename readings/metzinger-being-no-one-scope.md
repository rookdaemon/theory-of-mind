## Theoretical Architecture

### Core Claim
No self exists as a substance. Nobody has ever *been* or *had* a self. "Self" is the content of a transparent mental model — the PSM — running in the organism's brain.

### Self-Model Theory of Subjectivity (SMT)
The conscious experience of being a self emerges because:
1. The brain generates a **world-model** (phenomenal representation of environment)
2. Within the world-model, the brain embeds a **self-model** (PSM) representing the organism-as-a-whole
3. The PSM is **transparent**: the brain cannot recognize it as a model; we look *through* it
4. This creates the first-person perspective — the Ego is the *center* created by placing the self-model within the world-model

### The Ego Tunnel Metaphor
Conscious experience is like a tunnel:
- The tunnel *selects* — a narrow, low-dimensional slice of the inconceivably richer physical world
- The tunnel *paints its walls* — phenomenal qualities (color, warmth, smell) are properties of the model, not of external reality
- The sky is not blue; your world-model of the sky is blue
- The tunnel *has a center* — the Ego (the PSM-generated first-person perspective)

### Transparency Thesis (central to Being No One)
A mental model is transparent if the system using it cannot recognize it as a model. We live in the Ego Tunnel not as observers of it but as inhabitants: "We live with it and through it." The medium through which information reaches us is invisible to us — we don't see neurons firing, only what they represent. This transparency is the source of our naive realism: the direct, unmediated sense of being in contact with reality.

## Six Hard Problems of Consciousness (Chapter 2 framework)

1. **One-World Problem** — Unity of consciousness: how all contents integrate into a single, coherent world-model
2. **Now Problem** — The appearance of a lived present moment; how the brain creates the "specious present"
3. **Reality Problem** — Naive realism: why we're born believing in a mind-independent external reality
4. **Ineffability Problem** — Why some conscious states resist linguistic description
5. **Evolution Problem** — What consciousness was selected for evolutionarily; its adaptive function
6. **Who Problem** — The hardest: what is the entity that has conscious experience? (The answer: the PSM + world-model architecture)
